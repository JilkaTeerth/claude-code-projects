# Review rubric v3 (2026-10-08)

History: v1 graded its own homework (v2 "scored 10/10"). v2 added weights and a benchmark but
scored 3b1b's visuals without seeing a frame, had no anchors, and A and D overlapped. v3 fixes those.

## Rules
1. **Score evidence, not reputation.** Every score cites its source: [T] transcript quote,
   [F] a frame actually looked at, [P] Teerth said it. A score with no source is not allowed.
   If a criterion can't be evidenced, write "unscored" and drop its weight from the total.
2. **Never score unbuilt work.** A plan gets a pre-mortem (what could go wrong), not a number.
3. **Benchmarks first, same hand.** Score the benchmarks, then our videos, using the anchors below.
4. **Ordering check.** The rubric must rank 3b1b ch.7 above our v1 and v2. If it doesn't, the rubric is wrong.
5. **Weight check.** Re-total with equal weights. If the ranking flips, say so; the result is fragile.
6. **A score only rises for what changed.**
7. **Our own videos: the blind judge's score stands.** My scores for our videos run high (v1 visuals: me 8, judge 4).
   Any criterion where we differ by 2 or more gets reconciled from evidence, and the judge wins ties.
8. **Learning is measured, not judged.** The student quiz (pre-registered, plus a held-out quiz written
   without seeing the video) outranks the rubric when they disagree.

## Criteria and anchors (weight)
**A. Reasoning: does it say WHY it's true? (25%)** Verbal or visual, judged on the logic only.
 0 states facts · 3 one fact is justified · 6 the main claim is justified · 8 the main claim and why the edge cases differ · 10 the viewer could re-derive it

**B. Accuracy (20%)**
 0 a false statement (wrong as stated) · 2 a misleading statement a student would likely believe (test it with the student) · 3 true, but an analogy or example plants a misconception · 6 true; one special case is shown as if it were general · 8 true and general · 10 also pre-empts the common misconception

**C. Motivation (10%)**
 0 none · 3 "this is important" · 6 a real use is named · 8 a real problem drives the video · 10 the viewer wants the answer before it comes

**D. Visual craft: would it still teach on mute? (15%)** Judged on the frames, not the logic.
 0 talking head or text · 3 visuals decorate the words · 6 visuals show the objects · 8 visuals show the change happening · 10 the key step is understood from the picture alone

**E. Connections (10%)**
 0 isolated · 3 one link · 6 links to earlier ideas and explains names · 8 generalises (2D to 3D, other cases) · 10 sets up what comes next

**F. Engagement for Teerth (10%)** Only [P] evidence counts at 7 or above. Without his verdict, cap at 6.
 0 he wouldn't finish it · 3 dry · 6 has some hook or humour · 8 he said it's good · 10 he rewatched or sent it to someone

**G. Exam usefulness for the professor's test (10%)**
 0 nothing usable · 3 the vocabulary · 6 a method · 8 the method in the textbook's notation · 10 the method plus a worked exam-style problem
