## Narration (everything the viewer hears)

Okay. Your roommate just built the dumbest machine on campus. A sandwich press... so strong... it flattens anything. He puts in a burger. Out comes... a burger pancake.

He puts in a basketball. Out comes a sad, orange circle. Then he puts in his phone. Now he owns a very expensive coaster.

But here's the weird part. Look at the burger pancake. Is it still wide? Yep. Still deep? Yep. But how tall is it? Zero. The up-down direction... got completely crushed.

So let's count. The burger lived in three directions. One got crushed. Two made it out alive. One plus two... is three. Nothing just vanished. Every direction either got crushed, or survived.

And that... honestly... is the whole Dimension Theorem. Mathematicians just gave the press a fancier name. A linear transformation. Everything that gets crushed to zero is called the kernel. Everything that comes out the other side is called the range.

Now a real one, from your class. This matrix, one, two, two, four, is a press for the flat plane. Watch it squash the whole world.

Everything lands on one line. That line is the range. One direction survived. So the range has dimension one.

Now watch this arrow. Negative two, one. Does it land on the line somewhere? Nope. It lands on zero. Crushed... just like the burger's height. That whole red line is the kernel. Dimension one.

One crushed. One survived. One plus one is two... the dimension we started with. Written properly, the dimension of the kernel, plus the dimension of the range, equals the dimension of V.

On the test, skip the pictures. Row reduce. Count the pivots. That's the range. Count the free variables. That's the kernel. Together? They always add up to the number of columns.

So next time a scary transformation shows up on your exam... just ask yourself... what got pancaked? And what made it out alive?
