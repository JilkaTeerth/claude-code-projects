You asked about this

How safe each one actually is

You heard Colombia is unsafe. That's broadly right on paper: the US State Department has Colombia at Level 3, reconsider travel, and Medellín is dealing with a scopolamine drugging surge aimed at foreigners. That doesn't make Cartagena off limits, but it does mean the cheapest fun on this board comes with real rules attached.

What the levels mean: Level 1 is normal precautions, Level 2 is increased caution, Level 3 is reconsider travel, Level 4 is do not travel. Ratings are national, so a Level 3 country can still have tourist districts that are busy and policed. US destinations get no rating at all, which is not the same as no crime.

The rules that actually keep us safe

  • Watch every drink get poured, and never take one from a stranger. This is the single biggest risk in Cartagena and Medellín.
  • Nobody leaves a bar with someone they met that night. Drugging robberies almost always start that way.
  • Taxis or Uber after dark, never walking back, in Negril, Cartagena and Medellín.
  • Split cash, cards and a phone between two people. Carry an old phone on Rio's beaches.
  • Stay in the zones named on each card. The ratings are about places we won't be going.
  • Share locations in the group chat for the week, and agree nobody walks off alone.
The boring but important bit

Keep the house clean, get through the airport

Pick a place with a housekeeper$0, tip ~$60

Common in Cartagena colonial houses and Negril villas. Filter Airbnb for "housekeeper" or "cleaning included."

Or book 3 cleaner visits$25 to $70 each

Ask the host before paying: "Can your cleaner come Mon, Wed, Fri?" Get it in the Airbnb chat. Already counted in every price here.

Or go all-inclusive$110 to $190 pp/night

Daily housekeeping and open bar, but we lose a walkable neighbourhood. Punta Cana, Cabo, Vallarta, Nassau and Negril.

House kitty$40 pp

Covers cleaner tips, trash bags and the first grocery and alcohol run.

Expired F-1 stamp? For trips under 30 days, Mexico, DR, Jamaica and the Bahamas let F-1 students back into the US on an expired stamp (automatic revalidation). But Mexico, DR and the Bahamas need a valid US visa to let you in, so with an expired stamp only Jamaica works. Confirm with the DSO.

Everyone's checklist
  1. Passport valid past Sep 22, 2027
  2. F-1 stamp valid on Mar 21, 2027 (unless going to Jamaica)
  3. I-20 travel signature from F&M international student services, within 12 months
  4. Brazil only: consular visa, apply 8+ weeks ahead
